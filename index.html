Jupiter Swap, minus the guesswork

A redesign of the Jupiter swap interaction — the single most-used flow on Solana — focused on one problem: the current card makes you decode the trade before you can trust it.

What changed: one hero number (the guaranteed minimum), impact as a colored gauge not a decimal, fees collapsed to a single all-in figure, route stated in words. The CTA itself now repeats the trade — the last thing you read before committing is the trade, not the word "Swap".

1 · Lead with the guaranteed minimum, not the estimate

Today the big number is an estimate and the protection ("min. received") is 6px footnote text. Inverted: the minimum is the only number the protocol actually honors, so it moves into the headline — "You get — at least 82.98 USDC". The estimate stays, visually secondary. A newcomer can't be MEV'd or slip-spiked without seeing the worst case.

2 · Price impact as a gauge with a verdict

"0.12%" requires knowing what a good number is. A small green-to-red bar plus a plain-word verdict ("negligible / moderate / high") communicates safety at a glance, and turns red attractively early when a thin pool would eat the trade.

3 · One fee, in dollars

Platform fee + LP fee + network fee + priority fee as separate micro-numbers is accountant UX. Traders care about one thing: total cost. Collapsed to "$0.09 all-in", with an expandable line-item view on tap for power users.

4 · Route in words, not just a diagram

"2 hops" is meaningless to a newcomer. "Direct route · Orca → USDC, 1 hop" names the venues. The interactive route diagram stays one tap away, but the default answer to "where is my money going" is plain English.

5 · The CTA is the trade

"Swap" could send anything. "Swap 0.5 SOL → ~83.47 USDC" makes the button the final confirmation of the exact trade — a free, zero-friction guard against reviewing the wrong numbers, and it carries the guaranteed minimum in its second line.

6 · Slippage demoted to a locked outcome

Slippage settings are an expert control; the thing a normal user needs is the outcome: "worst case locked". The redesign shows the locked floor in the green strip and keeps the slippage gear as an advanced affordance, not a co-equal row.

Write-up: the problem and the bet

PAIN 01

Equal weight for unequal facts

Price impact, fees, route and minimum-received share one 11px gray line. The numbers that protect the user have the same visual rank as the ones that describe it. New users either ignore all of them or trust all of them.

PAIN 02

The estimate is the hero

The biggest number on screen is the one number the protocol does not guarantee. When a trade settles at 82.60 instead of 83.47, it feels like the app lied — because the app put the promise in the footnote.

PAIN 03

Settings pose as safety

"Auto 0.1%" slippage sits at the bottom like a seatbelt, but it's a preference. Users believe they're protected because a setting exists; nobody checks whether 0.1% suits a volatile pair.

PAIN 04

Generic confirmation

The button says "Swap" regardless of the trade above it. Any mismatch between what you reviewed and what executes passes unnoticed at the exact moment of commitment.

The bet: a swap card that reads like a receipt — "you pay X, you get at least Y, it costs Z, here's where it goes" — is simultaneously safer for newcomers and faster for experts. Every change above removes a decoding step, not a feature: the route diagram, fee breakdown and slippage tuning all remain one tap away. Jupiter's aggregation engine is the best in the ecosystem; this redesign just lets the interface speak its guarantees as clearly as the engine delivers them.

What I'd test next: whether the echoed-trade CTA reduces mis-signed swaps in practice (measurable via reversal rate), and whether the plain-word impact verdict ("negligible") increases completion on volatile pairs without increasing support tickets.
